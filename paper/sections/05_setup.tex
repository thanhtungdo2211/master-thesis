\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Camera-based partition.} Each image carries the identifier of the camera that captured it, so assigning every camera to one client partitions the \numTotImg{} training images into fifteen clients in a way that mirrors how surveillance data is actually owned; the server retains the $200$ test identities. The partition is non-IID in three specific respects, detailed per client in \cref{tab:partition} of \cref{app:tables}. Client sizes range from \numImgMin{} to \numImgMax{} images, a ratio of \numImgRatio{} with a coefficient of variation of \numCVSize, against below $0.3$ for the Dirichlet splits normally used to simulate non-IID federation. The cameras separate into two groups that share almost no identities --- Group~A with six cameras and \numShareA\% of the data, Group~B with eight and \numShareB\%, bridged only by camera~$4$ --- so that identity containment reaches $0.92$--$0.98$ within a group but falls to $0.02$ across groups, making the skew a domain skew inside each group and a near-complete label skew between them. Finally, since a client performs $\lfloor n_{k}/B\rfloor$ steps per round and is then weighted by $n_{k}/N$, its influence on the global update grows roughly as $n_{k}^{2}$~\citep{wang2020tacklingobjectiveinconsistencyproblem}: the two largest clients rise from \numTwoShareNom\% of the data to \numTwoShareEff\% of the update while the smallest falls from \numNomTwo\% to \numEffTwo\%, a contraction by a factor of \numSqueeze.

\paragraph{Scope of the measurements.} Two settings are compared in \cref{sec:results}: a \emph{centralized} reference, in which the whole training set is held by one client so that aggregation becomes the identity map and each round reduces to one epoch of ordinary training, and a \emph{federated} setting over the fifteen camera clients, all participating in every round. A full account of the training configuration is deferred to a later revision of this report, together with the settings still to be evaluated --- low-rank adaptation at other ranks, and CUHK-PEDES and ICFG-PEDES as additional benchmarks. Two caveats apply throughout: the federated run stopped before its curve had flattened, and every run uses a single seed.
