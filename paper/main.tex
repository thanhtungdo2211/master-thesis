\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Foundation models pre-trained at scale are now the default starting point for vision and language tasks, but adapting one by full fine-tuning updates hundreds of millions of parameters. Federated learning pays that cost repeatedly rather than once, since the data cannot be pooled and every client transmits its update in every round: full fine-tuning of a CLIP-scale backbone would require on the order of \numUplinkFull\,MiB per client per round. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning, which freezes the backbone and trains a small inserted delta, is the natural remedy, and this thesis investigates how such techniques behave once training is federated. We pursue the question on text-based person search, which retrieves images of a pedestrian from a gallery given a natural-language description. The task is a fitting instance: its galleries are genuinely distributed across surveillance installations whose operators cannot pool their imagery, its accuracy is dominated by CLIP-scale backbones, and yet every parameter-efficient method proposed for it assumes centralized training. This report documents the state of an ongoing thesis addressing that intersection. We review the literature with the federated constraint in view and identify the configuration that transfers --- a frozen CLIP backbone with the parameter-free Similarity Distribution Matching objective --- then describe a baseline pipeline instantiating it, namely CLIP ViT-B/16 adapted by LoRA of rank four and aggregated by FedAvg, on RSTPReid partitioned into fifteen camera-level clients. The adaptation trains \numTrainPct\% of the backbone parameters and transmits \numUplinkLoRA\,MiB per client per round. Trained centrally it converges at \numCPlatR{} Rank-1 accuracy and \numCPlatM{} mAP, \numGapIrraConvR{} points of Rank-1 below full fine-tuning with three objectives when both are read on a converged rather than a best-of-$N$ basis, and the federated runs approach this reference as rounds accumulate. The camera partition proves non-IID in two distinct regimes. We state which conclusions the present evidence supports, which remain open, and derive the experimental agenda from the latter.
\end{abstract}
